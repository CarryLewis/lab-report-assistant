Analysis of the Bacterial Growth Curve

Student: Sample Student　·　Course: Biotechnology Laboratory　·　2026

1. Introduction

Bacterial growth in a closed system typically follows a characteristic pattern consisting of lag, exponential, stationary, and death phases (Madigan et al., 2018). Understanding these phases is important for designing experiments and interpreting changes in cell population over time.

The objective of this experiment was to monitor the growth of Escherichia coli in nutrient broth by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD₆₀₀) at regular intervals.

2. Materials and Methods

An overnight culture was diluted into fresh nutrient broth and incubated at 37 °C with shaking. Samples were collected every 30 minutes for four hours. Cell density was estimated using a spectrophotometer at 600 nm, with sterile broth used as the blank.

Each measurement was performed once, and the culture was returned to the incubator immediately after sampling.

3. Results

OD₆₀₀ increased from 0.08 at the start of incubation to 0.72 after 180 minutes. The steepest increase was observed between 60 and 120 minutes. After 150 minutes, the increase became less pronounced.

The report describes a transition toward a stationary phase, although no replicate measurements or error bars are presented.

4. Discussion

The observed increase in optical density is consistent with bacterial growth in nutrient-rich medium. The slower increase near the end of the experiment may indicate nutrient limitation or accumulation of metabolic waste products (Monod, 1949).

Because the experiment was performed without replicates, random measurement variation cannot be estimated (Lee, 2020). In addition, the report does not explain whether the instrument was calibrated before each measurement.

5. Conclusion

The culture showed an increase in OD₆₀₀ over the observation period, with a reduced growth rate toward the end. Replicate measurements would improve confidence in the estimated growth pattern.
